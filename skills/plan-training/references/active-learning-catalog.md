# Evidence-Based Active Learning Catalog (21 Strategies from Tanner 2013)

This catalog summarizes evidence-based pedagogical strategies for higher education and STEM classrooms, adapted from Kimberly Tanner's *Structure Matters: Twenty-One Teaching Strategies that Promote Learner Engagement and Cultivate Classroom Equity* (CBE—Life Sciences Education, 2013).

---

## Category 1: Giving Learners Time to Think

1. **Wait Time (3–5 seconds)**: After asking a question, explicitly pause and wait at least 3–5 seconds before calling on any learner. Prevents the fastest responders from dominating and allows deep thinkers and non-native speakers to formulate responses.
2. **Allow Learners to Write Before Speaking**: Give learners 60 seconds to jot down their thoughts or calculations before sharing with the class. Writing clarifies thinking and increases willingness to speak.
3. **Think-Pair-Share (TPS)**:
   - *Think*: Learners work silently for 1–2 minutes on a challenging prompt.
   - *Pair*: Turn to an adjacent peer to compare reasoning for 2–3 minutes.
   - *Share*: Instructor samples responses across the classroom.

---

## Category 2: Encouraging, Demanding, and Actively Managing Participation

4. **Multiple Hands, Multiple Voices**: When asking a question to the room, wait until at least 4–5 hands are raised before calling on anyone. *"I see two hands; I'll wait until I see at least four hands before picking someone."*
5. **Random Calling with Notice ("Fair Sticks / Index Cards")**: Draw learner names randomly from a deck of cards or digital shuffler, but give learners notice and permission to "pass" or "ask a peer for assistance". Removes bias and broadens the circle of engagement.
6. **Assigning Classroom Roles in Groups**: When learners work in groups of 3–4, assign explicit roles:
   - *Manager*: Keeps track of time and ensures everyone contributes.
   - *Recorder / Coder*: Writes the code/solution document.
   - *Skeptic / Reporter*: Challenges assumptions and presents findings to the class.
7. **Round-Robin Sharing in Small Groups**: In teams, each member takes an uninterrupted 60-second turn to share their perspective before open debate begins. Ensures introverted learners are heard.

---

## Category 3: Building Community & Connecting to Prior Knowledge

8. **Icebreakers & Low-Stakes Questions**: Open class with a low-stakes conceptual poll or real-world observation to lower social barriers and build psychological safety.
9. **Identity Wheel / Inclusive Examples**: Intentionally highlight diverse scientists, practitioners, and global case studies. Avoid culture-bound idioms or exclusionary references.
10. **Pre-Assessment / Diagnostic Polling**: Spend the first 5 minutes diagnosing prior knowledge or exposing misconceptions before introducing a new equation or model.
11. **Connecting to Daily Experience**: Ground abstract formulas in physical intuition or everyday phenomena (e.g., explaining radiative equilibrium via a greenhouse, car interior on a sunny day, or thermal blankets).

---

## Category 4: Deepening Conceptual Understanding & Collaboration

12. **Concept Tests & Peer Instruction (Eric Mazur / Carl Wieman)**:
    - Pose a conceptual multiple-choice question targeting a common misconception.
    - Learners vote individually (Clickers / Polling / Slido).
    - If 30%–70% answer correctly: tell learners to find someone with a *different* answer and convince them.
    - Learners re-vote. The peer discussion almost universally increases accuracy and solidifies understanding.
    - Instructor debriefs the underlying rationale.
13. **Jigsaw Activity**:
    - Assign 4 different related topics (e.g., 4 planetary climate mechanisms: albedo, greenhouse gases, solar variability, aerosol scattering).
    - *Expert Groups*: Learners with the same topic meet to master it.
    - *Home Groups*: Regroup so each team has one expert from each topic. Each learner teaches their piece to the home group.
14. **Strip Sequences / Order-of-Operations Sorting**: Give learners a jumbled set of physical processes, algorithm steps, or derivation lines on strips of paper (or digital cards) and ask them to reconstruct the correct sequence in pairs.
15. **Concept Mapping**: Learners collaboratively draw diagrams connecting equations, physical mechanisms, and real-world observations with labeled directional arrows.

---

## Category 5: Monitoring Learning & Gathering Immediate Feedback

16. **Minute Paper**: At the end of class, ask learners to write for 1 minute: *"What was the most important thing you learned today?"*
17. **Muddiest Point Card**: Ask learners: *"What concept, derivation, or problem from today's lecture remains least clear or most confusing to you?"* Review these before the next lecture to address sticking points.
18. **One-Sentence Summary**: Ask learners to distill a 50-minute lecture topic into a single syntactically correct sentence answering *Who, What, Why, When, and How*.
19. **Misconception Check**: Present a plausible but subtly incorrect derivation or physics explanation and ask learners to locate and correct the error.
20. **Pass the Problem**: In groups of 3, Group A begins solving a complex coding/derivation problem for 3 minutes, then passes it to Group B to continue/debug, and so on.
21. **Debriefing the Process**: Explicitly ask learners to reflect on *how* they solved a problem, highlighting effective study habits and metacognitive strategies.
