# Learner-Centered Syllabus Construction: Rubric and Template

Adapted from the **MIT Kaufman Teaching Certificate Program (KTCP)** Workshop on Syllabus Construction, this guide contrasts traditional teacher-centered syllabi with evidence-based learner-centered syllabi.

---

## 1. Content-Centered vs. Learner-Centered Syllabus

| Dimension | Content-Centered (Traditional) | Learner-Centered (Evidence-Based) |
|---|---|---|
| **Tone** | Regulatory, punitive, legalistic (*"Learners will be penalized if..."*) | Inviting, collaborative, supportive (*"In this course, we will discover together..."*) |
| **Focus** | Course content, textbook chapters, instructor authority | Learner learning outcomes, intellectual growth, pathways to success |
| **Learning Outcomes** | Vague or absent (*"We will cover thermodynamics"*) | Explicit, measurable action verbs mapped to Bloom's taxonomy |
| **Assessment & Grading** | Arbitrary point totals with penalties | Transparent rubrics, specifications criteria, and revision policies |
| **Policies** | Rigid prohibitions and disclaimers | Clear rationale for expectations, wellness resources, accommodation support |

---

## 2. Syllabus Construction Rubric (KTCP Criteria)

Use this rubric to evaluate and polish any course syllabus:

### Criterion 1: Welcoming & Supportive Tone
- Uses personal, inclusive pronouns (*we, you, our*).
- Expresses genuine enthusiasm for the discipline and belief in all learners' capacity to succeed (Growth Mindset).
- Explicitly frames office hours as an open, welcoming consultation space.

### Criterion 2: Measurable Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)
- Explicitly articulated on the first or second page.
- Employs measurable action verbs (Analyze, Evaluate, Design, Solve).
- Directly maps to the course's position in the broader curriculum.

### Criterion 3: Constructive Alignment Matrix
- Clear schedule linking weekly topics, pre-class preparation, in-class active sessions, and graded deliverables.
- Explicit rationale provided for each assignment and reading.

### Criterion 4: Transparent Grading & Evaluation Criteria
- Clear breakdown of how grades are earned.
- Detailed rubrics or criteria sheets for major assignments and projects.
- Explicit policy on resubmissions, late work flexibility, or dropped lowest scores.

### Criterion 5: Inclusivity, Accessibility & Support Policies
- **Disability & Accommodation Statement**: Proactive, welcoming invitation to arrange academic accommodations.
- **Mental Health & Well-being Resources**: Direct contact links to learner counseling, wellness centers, and dean's office.
- **Diversity, Equity & Inclusion Statement**: Commitment to respecting diverse backgrounds, identities, and viewpoints.
- **Academic Integrity & Collaboration Policy**: Clear boundaries explaining when collaboration is encouraged (e.g., study groups, concept discussions) vs. when individual work is required (e.g., exams, final write-ups), framed around ethical development rather than surveillance.

## How We Will Learn: Active Classroom Environment

This course is designed around evidence-based active learning. Instead of passive lecturing, class time will be dedicated to:
- Interactive discussions, **Think-Pair-Share** problem-solving, and live coding.
- Conceptual peer instruction using live polls.
- Collaborative small-group analysis of real-world datasets.

Please come to class prepared to engage, ask questions, and collaborate with your peers.

---

## Assessment & Grading Philosophy

Learning is an iterative process. Our assessment structure is designed to provide frequent, low-stakes feedback so you can continuously refine your understanding.

### Grading Breakdown
- **Active Class Engagement & Formative Checks**: 15%
- **Weekly Problem Sets & Code Laboratories**: 35%
- **Midterm Conceptual Evaluation**: 20%
- **Final Capstone Project & Presentation**: 30%

### Revision Policy
We believe in mastery. You may resubmit up to two weekly problem sets within one week of receiving feedback to earn back up to 85% of missed points by demonstrating improved understanding.

## Course Policies & Community Norms

### Academic Integrity & Ethical Collaboration
We strongly encourage studying and discussing concepts in groups! However, any code or written text you submit must represent your own original synthesis and execution. Always credit peers who contributed ideas to your discussions.

### Accommodations for Diverse Learning Needs
If you have a documented disability or require specific accommodations, please reach out to me early in the semester. I am fully committed to ensuring that all course materials and environments are accessible and supportive.

### Mental Health and Well-Being
Your mental, emotional, and physical health are paramount. If you are experiencing academic stress, personal difficulties, or exhaustion, please reach out. Campus counseling resources are available at: `[Campus Wellness Contact]`.
