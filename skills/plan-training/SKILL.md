# Evidence-Based Teaching & Course Design (KTCP Pedagogy)

## Overview

This skill synthesizes core pedagogical research and best practices from **MIT's Kaufman Teaching Certificate Program (KTCP)** into an actionable instructional design system for higher education and technical instruction.

The guiding philosophy is **Constructive Alignment and Learner-Centered Backward Design**: we begin with what learners should be able to _do_ (Intended Learning Outcomes), design authentic evidence to evaluate that mastery, and structure active, inclusive classroom experiences to facilitate deep learning.

---

## When to Use

- Designing a new course, syllabus, or modular curriculum.
- Outlining a 50-minute or 80-minute lesson plan with active learning cycles.
- Formulating precise Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs) using Bloom's Taxonomy.
- Integrating structured active learning (e.g., Think-Pair-Share, Concept Tests, Jigsaw).
- Building an inclusive, high-belonging classroom culture and mitigating stereotype threat.
- Developing transparent rubrics, specifications grading schemes, and actionable feedback loops.
- Preparing for microteaching demonstrations or conducting peer teaching observations.

## Pillar Breakdown & Key Research

### 1. Backward Course Design (Wiggins & McTighe 2005)

Start with the end in mind. Avoid the "twin sins" of instruction: _aimless activity-focused teaching_ (doing fun labs with no clear goal) and _coverage-focused teaching_ (racing through a textbook without verifying learner understanding).

1. **Stage 1 — Identify Desired Results**: Formulate measurable **Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)** using Bloom's Revised Taxonomy action verbs (_"By the end of this session, learners will be able to [Action Verb]..."_). Avoid unmeasurable verbs like _"understand"_, _"appreciate"_, or _"learn"_.
2. **Stage 2 — Determine Acceptable Evidence**: Design formative assessments (low-stakes checks during learning) and summative assessments (authentic tasks demonstrating mastery).
3. **Stage 3 — Plan Learning Experiences**: Select interactive, sequenced instructional activities that scaffold learners toward the assessment criteria.

See [references/backward-design-guide.md](references/backward-design-guide.md) for action verb tables and ILO matrices.

### 2. Teaching for Belonging & Inclusivity (Asai 2020, Rainey et al. 2018)

Learners learn best in an environment where they feel respected, valued, and psychologically safe.

- **Identity Awareness**: Recognize the diverse backgrounds, socio-economic contexts, and identities learners bring to the classroom (e.g., first-generation learners, international learners, underrepresented minorities).
- **Mitigate Stereotype Threat & Imposter Syndrome**: Normalize struggle as an inherent, healthy part of learning rather than a lack of innate ability.
- **Process Praise (Dwyer 2010)**: Praise effort, strategic problem-solving, and persistence (_"Your systematic error decomposition was effective"_) rather than innate intelligence (_"You are so smart at math"_), fostering a growth mindset.
- **Equitable Classroom Participation**: Never rely solely on calling on the first raised hands (which privileges the most extroverted/confident). Use **Think-Pair-Share**, **anonymous polling**, or **structured random calling** with advance notice.

---

### 3. Active Learning & Facilitation (Tanner 2013, Felder & Brent 2016, Freeman et al. 2014)

Extensive meta-analyses show that active learning decreases failure rates in STEM by 55% compared to pure traditional lecturing.

- **The 15-Minute Rule**: Cognitive retention plummets after 10–15 minutes of uninterrupted lecturing. Break every class into 10–15 minute instructional chunks followed by an active learner retrieval/application task.
- **High-Impact Active Learning Techniques**:
  - **Think-Pair-Share (TPS)**: Individual reflection (1–2 min) $\to$ Peer discussion (2–3 min) $\to$ Whole-class debrief (3–5 min).
  - **Peer Instruction / Concept Tests (Mazur / Wieman)**: Pose a conceptual multiple-choice question $\to$ Solo vote $\to$ Peer debate with adjacent learner $\to$ Re-vote $\to$ Instructor-led synthesis.
  - **Jigsaw**: Break a complex problem into 4 sub-topics. Expert groups master one part, then regroup into mixed teams where each learner teaches their sub-topic to their peers.
  - **Minute Paper / Muddiest Point**: 2-minute exit ticket: _"What was the most critical concept learned today, and what remains most confusing?"_

See [references/active-learning-catalog.md](references/active-learning-catalog.md) for 21 detailed classroom activity protocols.

### 4. Scaffolding & Cognitive Load Management (Ambrose & Lovett 2014)

Novices and experts organize knowledge differently. Experts chunk information automatically; novices can be overwhelmed by cognitive load.

- **Diagnose & Activate Prior Knowledge**: Begin every topic by connecting to familiar analogies, real-world observations, or previous units.
- **Bridge Misconceptions**: Explicitly surface common false intuitions (e.g., _"Summer occurs because Earth is closer to the Sun"_), test them with empirical counter-examples, and rebuild the correct conceptual model.
- **Fading Scaffolds**: Provide high structural support initially (e.g., worked examples, partially completed code skeletons), then systematically reduce support as learners build competence.

---

### 5. Actionable Feedback & Transparent Grading (Ambrose et al. 2010, Nilson, Schinske & Tanner)

- **4 Characteristics of Effective Feedback**:
  1. **Goal-Directed**: Tied explicitly to the stated ILO.
  2. **Targeted**: Specific about what was correct/incorrect.
  3. **Timely**: Delivered while there is still opportunity for learners to apply it.
  4. **Actionable**: Tells the learner _how_ to improve their approach on the next iteration.
- **Specifications & Mastery Grading**: Replace opaque partial-credit point-deductions with clear binary specifications (Satisfactory / Revision Needed). Bundle assignments into transparent grade tiers (A, B, C) and grant "revision tokens" to reward mastery over speed.
- **Avoid Curved Grading**: Curved grading creates artificial scarcity, disincentivizes peer collaboration, and measures relative ranking rather than actual learning.

See [references/feedback-grading-guide.md](references/feedback-grading-guide.md) for rubric design and feedback rubrics.

### 6. Learner-Centered Syllabus Construction

A syllabus is a learning guide and invitation, not a legal contract.

- **Tone**: Warm, supportive, and collaborative (_"In this course, we will explore..."_ vs. _"Learners are required to..."_).
- **Core Components**:
  - Welcoming course description & instructor positionality.
  - Explicit Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs).
  - Weekly schedule with constructive alignment (Topics $\leftrightarrow$ Reading $\leftrightarrow$ Formative Checks $\leftrightarrow$ Deliverables).
  - Transparent grading policy and rubric breakdowns.
  - Comprehensive wellness, accessibility/disability accommodation, and academic integrity statements.

See [references/learner-centered-syllabus.md](references/learner-centered-syllabus.md) for the complete rubric and template.

## 50-Minute Active Lecture Template

Use this standard template to design an engaging class session:

| Time          | Phase                       | Instructor Action                                                                                   | Learner Action                                  |
| ------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **00–05 min** | **Hook & Recall**           | Present intriguing real-world paradox or question; activate prior knowledge.                        | Think individually; respond via live poll.      |
| **05–18 min** | **Mini-Lecture 1**          | Introduce core concept / physical model; unpack key equations or mechanism.                         | Active note-taking; identify questions.         |
| **18–28 min** | **Active Learning Task**    | Facilitate **Think-Pair-Share** or **Concept Test**; circulate the room to diagnose misconceptions. | Work in pairs; debate and reach consensus.      |
| **28–38 min** | **Mini-Lecture 2**          | Deconstruct the peer activity findings; extend to real-world edge cases or data.                    | Connect activity results to the broader theory. |
| **38–46 min** | **Application / Problem**   | Assign a small-group problem or code exercise.                                                      | Collaborate in groups of 3; derive solution.    |
| **46–50 min** | **Synthesis & Exit Ticket** | Summarize core takeaways; collect "Muddiest Point" 1-minute card.                                   | Complete exit ticket to solidify learning.      |
