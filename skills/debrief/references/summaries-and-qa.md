# Summaries and Q&A

Reached from skill `debrief`; use this file when you shape a summary slide or prepare for questions and answers (Q&A) in a meeting.

## Upgrade Raw Data

| Raw input                           | Show it as                                  | Why                                                     |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| A table of absolute amounts         | Growth rates and shares of the total        | Readers compare change and mix, which raw totals hide.  |
| A long list of trade-offs           | A two-by-two matrix with two named axes     | Four cells show which option wins on each axis.         |
| A step-by-step narrative of options | An options-by-criteria scorecard up front   | The reader sees the verdict first, then the reasons.    |
| A slide crowded with many metrics   | One chart per slide with a conclusion title | One message per slide is easier to recall and to check. |

## Test the Titles

- Weak: "Delivery delays", a topic label that makes no claim.
- Strong: "Late deliveries fell from 9% to 6% after we added a second southern carrier."
- Rule: read only the titles, in order. They must tell the whole story. If they do not, rewrite the titles before you touch the charts.

## Answer a Question in Four Moves

1. Answer in the first sentence with yes, no or the number, without repeating the question.
2. Connect the answer to one of your main points.
3. Give one concrete fact or a short comparison as proof.
4. Stop. Do not add trailing caveats.

## Hard Questions

- Do not repeat a hostile premise. Restate the goal or the facts in neutral words, then answer.
- When data is missing, say what is known, what is not, and the next check that settles it.

## Align Stakeholders

- Keep answers brief but not curt: one clause of context, then the answer and the next need.
- Adjust the register by audience: technical detail with peers, more framing with executives and outside partners.
- Anchor on shared goals, separate people from options, and price each trade-off in cost, time and risk before you recommend.
