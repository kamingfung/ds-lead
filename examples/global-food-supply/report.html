1 · Aggregate supply

Global cereal output grew 257% since 1961, driven 90% by yields rather than land

World cereal production, yield, harvested area, and population, Index (1961 = 100)

Key takeaways

  • Beat population: Output grew 257% vs. 164% population (1961–2023)1,2.
  • Land spared: Cereal area rose 14%; yield rose 212% to 4,228 kg/ha kg/ha1.
  • Capital signal: Seed and soil productivity beat broad-acre expansion.
Assumptions. Yield share uses logarithmic growth decomposition: ln(yield ratio) / ln(production ratio) over 1961–2023, because 2024 historical population is not yet populated in the cereal series.

Source: 1 FAOSTAT Crops; 2 UN WPP.

2 · Regional divergence

Per-capita caloric supply rose everywhere, yet Africa sits 450 kcal/day below the global average

Select a region above to inspect its 1961–2023 caloric gain and 2024 undernourishment rate.

Daily per-capita dietary energy supply by FAO region, kcal / person / day (1961–2023)

Key takeaways

  • Asian catch-up: Asia added 1,186 kcal/day since 1961, reaching 2,991 kcal/day3.
  • African lag: Africa rose 563 to 2,556 kcal/day, 450 behind the world3.
  • Demographic drag: Rapid population growth absorbs output before buffers form.
Assumptions and limitations. FAO Food Balance Sheets measure national food supply available at retail level (kcal/person/day), not actual household caloric intake.

Source: 3 FAOSTAT Food Balance Sheets.

3 · The access gap

Undernourishment fell globally to 8.2% in 2024, while Africa stayed at 20.2%

Prevalence of undernourishment (SDG 2.1.1) by FAO region, Percent of population (2024)

Key takeaways

  • Global drop: World rate fell 4.5 pp pp, from 12.7% (2000) to 8.2% (2024)4.
  • Africa outlier: Sub-Saharan Africa is 22.3%, nearly triple the global rate4.
  • Access barriers: Price spikes and storage losses block food access.
Assumptions and limitations. FAO reports prevalence below 2.5% at the floor value of 2.5% (Europe). Undernourishment measures habitual dietary energy inadequacy.

Source: 4 FAO SOFI / SDG 2.1.1.

Redirect 2027–2030 capital from aggregate acreage to African yield-gap closure and storage
